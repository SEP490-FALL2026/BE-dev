## Context

Earlier slices and ADR-0008 assumed an organization can have several Super Admins and protected it with a **count**: _the last usable Super Admin cannot be locked or lose the role_ (`QĐ-39c(2)`). On 2026-10-03 the Owner decided otherwise (`QĐ-44`): each organization has **at most one** account holding `SUPER_ADMIN` at any time — an invariant of the data, not only of the seed.

- With one holder, "is this the last one?" is always true; the guard and its count (`countOtherActiveSuperAdmins`, the _usable_ filter, error `IDENTITY.LAST_SUPER_ADMIN`) have no meaning.
- `QĐ-39c(1)` forbids anyone to change their **own** roles. The sole Super Admin therefore cannot hand the role over with the generic grant/revoke endpoints, and nobody else can be a Super Admin to do it for them. A dedicated, tightly bounded operation is the only in-application way (`QĐ-44c`).
- Locking an account, or terminating its employee, must **not** free the role (`QĐ-44b`): otherwise "no usable Super Admin" would invite granting the role outside the process. _Holding_ and _being usable_ are different facts.
- Offline recovery (`QĐ-41b`) still exists for a holder who is no longer usable, but with one holder it must treat the **old grant and the new grant** consistently (`QĐ-44d`).
- With native passwords the holder has a **credential** the organization cannot reset on their behalf. A holder who is still ACTIVE but cannot sign in (lost password and mailbox) is a third, distinct problem: not a transfer and not a role recovery. It is handled by `CREDENTIAL` recovery (decision 8, `QĐ-47a`).

Data facts relied on: `user_role_grants` carries `organization_id`; grants keep history with `revoked_at`; every identity write serializes on the per-organization `IDENTITY` advisory lock (native design §9.1); hand-written database constraints follow ADR-0005; Prisma 7.9.1 with `previewFeatures = ["partialIndexes"]` generates a partial unique index (verified in a scratch schema on 2026-10-04, native design §12 — drift behaviour on a real database is still a B1 acceptance item).

## Decision

1. **Invariant in the database.** One partial unique index on `user_role_grants (organization_id) WHERE role_code = 'SUPER_ADMIN' AND revoked_at IS NULL`, declared in the Prisma schema with `where: raw(...)` and stable mapped name `user_role_grants_one_active_super_admin_idx`, per ADR-0005. Review the generated migration SQL; hand-written preflight/constraints remain in that migration. Include a presence/drift test and a mutation check (drop the index ⟹ the test that inserts a second grant goes red). A unique violation is mapped to `409 IDENTITY.SUPER_ADMIN_ALREADY_ASSIGNED`; behind the lock it should be unreachable, it is the last line of defence. The migration **pre-flights**: it aborts with a readable message if any organization already has more than one active `SUPER_ADMIN` grant (relevant to a shared database — `LR-14`).
2. **Three different facts about a holder.** _Holder_ = the account with the unrevoked `SUPER_ADMIN` grant. _Usable holder_ = holder ∧ account `ACTIVE` ∧ `activationState = ACTIVATED` ∧ credential present ∧ employee not `TERMINATED`. _Continuity states_ derived from these: `HEALTHY` (usable holder exists), `HOLDER_UNUSABLE` (holder exists, not usable, with a reason) and `NO_HOLDER` (no grant — an integrity incident, never a normal state). Locking the holder's account or terminating the holder's employee never touches the grant.
3. **Generic role endpoints never touch `SUPER_ADMIN`.** `POST /app-users` with `SUPER_ADMIN` among the roles, `POST /app-users/{id}/roles` with `SUPER_ADMIN`, and `DELETE /app-users/{id}/roles/SUPER_ADMIN` are refused with `409 IDENTITY.SUPER_ADMIN_TRANSFER_REQUIRED`. Check order: self-change (`403`) → assignable set and forbidden combination (`422`) → this check (`409`). The role enters an organization only through the bootstrap seed and changes holder only through decision 4 or the recovery of decision 8. (`POST …/lock` is not in this list: only the Super Admin can lock accounts and cannot lock their own, so a lock never reaches the holder.)
4. **Transfer** — `POST /app-users/{id}/super-admin-transfer` (`{id}` is the **successor**; `operationId` `identityTransferSuperAdmin`; guard `@RequireRoles('SUPER_ADMIN')`; body `{ currentPassword }`). One transaction:
   1. verify `currentPassword` against the actor's credential **before** opening the transaction (hashing never runs inside a transaction), subject to the reauthentication limits (5/min/account, 20/min/IP); a wrong `currentPassword` answers `400 CURRENT_PASSWORD_INVALID`, as `A08` (_annotation, COORDINATOR chốt kỹ thuật 04/10/2026 (trong phạm vi Owner giao ở QĐ-47; không phải quyết định nghiệp vụ); walkthrough W-10_);
   2. take the `IDENTITY` advisory lock **exclusive** (native design §9.1, the canonical order); row locks follow that fixed order — app_users → user_role_grants → auth_sessions/tokens → employees, ascending UUID within a table — so the actor's and the successor's `app_users` rows are locked together (`FOR UPDATE`, ascending UUID) **before** any `user_role_grants` row, and the employee row last; _(corrected 2026-10-04 to match design §9.1: the earlier wording omitted `user_role_grants` and locked the successor's `app_users` row after the actor's grant; no decision changed)_;
   3. re-read the actor (from the row locked in step 2): account `ACTIVE`, `ACTIVATED`, employee not `TERMINATED`, `credentialVersion` equal to the one the reauthentication was verified against, otherwise `403 ACCOUNT_LOCKED` / `403 ACCOUNT_HOLDER_TERMINATED` / `401` (the credential changed or the session was revoked while waiting — the session really is invalid);
   4. lock the actor's **active `SUPER_ADMIN` grant** `FOR UPDATE` ⟹ `403 FORBIDDEN` if the actor no longer holds it (a concurrent transfer won);
   5. successor: `app_users` row (already locked in step 2), grants (plain read — every writer of grants holds `IDENTITY` exclusive, so no second grant lock is needed; F5); employee `FOR SHARE` through the `organization` facade; eligibility checks of decision 5;
   6. **revoke the actor's grant first** (`revoked_by = actor`), **then insert the successor's grant** (`granted_by = actor`), so the unique index holds at every statement; the actor's sessions are **not** revoked by the transfer (they simply no longer carry the role from the next request);
   7. two audit rows in the same transaction, one per account, same `correlation_id`, actions `SUPER_ADMIN_TRANSFERRED_OUT` (object = former holder) and `SUPER_ADMIN_TRANSFERRED_IN` (object = new holder), `before`/`after` role sets, `after_state.transfer = { fromAppUserId, toAppUserId }`.
      Any failure rolls back everything: the actor is still the holder, the successor has no grant, no audit row.
5. **Successor eligibility** (all must hold; otherwise nothing changes):

   | Condition                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             | Refusal                                                                            |
   | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
   | successor exists in the organization                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | `404 IDENTITY.APP_USER_NOT_FOUND`                                                  |
   | successor ≠ actor                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     | `422 IDENTITY.TRANSFER_TARGET_INVALID`, `details.reason = SAME_ACCOUNT`            |
   | account `ACTIVE`                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      | `422 IDENTITY.TRANSFER_TARGET_INVALID`, `details.reason = ACCOUNT_LOCKED`          |
   | employee `status = ACTIVE` (not `LONG_LEAVE`, not in handover, not `TERMINATED`) and already started (`hireDate <= today`; a future start date gives `details.reason = NOT_YET_STARTED` — _annotation, W-22, COORDINATOR chốt kỹ thuật 04/10/2026 (trong phạm vi Owner giao ở QĐ-47; không phải quyết định nghiệp vụ)_) — Docs `PD-1` | `422 IDENTITY.EMPLOYEE_NOT_ACTIVE`, `details.employeeStatus`, `details.inHandover` |
   | account `ACTIVATED` with a credential, **and** `emailVerificationSource` present (`INVITATION_REDEEMED`, `BOOTSTRAP_ASSERTED` or `RECOVERY_REDEEMED`)                                                                                                                                                                                 | `409 IDENTITY.TRANSFER_TARGET_NOT_ACTIVATED`                                       |
   | account holds neither `IT_ADMIN` nor `FINANCE` (`C-17`)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               | `422 IDENTITY.ROLE_COMBINATION_FORBIDDEN`                                          |
   | employee holds none of the three approval roles (`C-18`; read from `approval_role_holders`, created by B1b, **inside the transfer transaction**, rows valid now; an empty result is trusted only when the query succeeded and a query error aborts the transfer — there is no default-empty provider)                                 | `409 IDENTITY.ROLE_HELD_BY_APPROVAL_HOLDER`                                        |

   The "activated" test replaces the earlier "identity bound explicitly" test (ADR-0010 decision 5): the check of the person's identity is not dropped, it is now the fact that this person redeemed an invitation sent to the account's own mailbox (or, for the bootstrap account, that the operator asserted it in an audited bootstrap).

6. **The self-change exception is structural.** `assertNotSelf` stays on every generic operation. Only this single code path may revoke the actor's **own** `SUPER_ADMIN` grant, and only together with granting the successor in the same transaction. It is not a parameter or flag of the generic role service. Tests prove both halves: the generic path still refuses (`US-01-01` AC-13, AC-14) and the transfer path cannot leave zero or two holders.
7. **Effect on the next request.** Roles are read from the database on every request; there is no cache. The former holder's next request — even with an unexpired access token — has no `SUPER_ADMIN`, so every route that requires it answers `403 FORBIDDEN`; if they have direct reports they regain the derived `MANAGER` role (ADR-0008 decision 1). The new holder has the role from their next request.
8. **Recovery treats old and new grants together** (`QĐ-44d`, `QĐ-41b`, `QĐ-47a`, `QĐ-47e`, `QĐ-47f`). Offline only: one command, `scripts/recover-super-admin.ts`, **no HTTP endpoint, no flag that skips authentication, bootstrap is never reused** (bootstrap refuses to run when an organization exists and touches no existing account). The authoritative procedure, inputs, states and tests are in [native design 6.2](../architecture/native-identity-workforce-design.md); the decision-level rules are:
   - Common: a written `approvalRef` unique per organization (table `recovery_operations`); shared environment: approver ≠ operator and recipient's employee ≠ operator, **approver ≠ recipient is not required**; local/test environments need no separation but are audited identically and marked `LOCAL_TEST`. The command checks **declared codes and the ticket reference only**; it cannot prove that the ticket is genuine or that the person at the keyboard is the declared operator — those are controlled outside the system, and neither the command output nor the documents may call separation "guaranteed". One transaction per phase under the `IDENTITY` exclusive lock; every account whose credential or role changes has all sessions and unredeemed tokens revoked (`RECOVERY`) and `credentialVersion` incremented; one audit row per such account with the same `approvalRef` and `correlation_id`; the operator never chooses, sees, prints or knows a password — the recipient sets their own through a one-time link sent by the normal outbox, and the command prints only the operation id, the status and a masked destination.
   - **`UNLOCK`** — holder account `LOCKED`, employee working: unlock only, no second grant.
   - **`REASSIGN`** — one two-phase procedure under one `approvalRef` (two phases and the atomic phase 2: `QĐ-47e`, derived from `QĐ-44d`/`QĐ-41b`); allowed only when there is no holder, the holder's employee is `TERMINATED`, or the holder's account is `LOCKED` and the recipient is a different person (aligned to `QĐ-44b(3)`/`QĐ-44d`; same recipient ⇒ `UNLOCK`). A holder unusable only because of `CREDENTIAL_RESET_PENDING` or `NOT_ACTIVATED` is refused by `REASSIGN` (`REASSIGN_NOT_APPLICABLE`, both phases): `CREDENTIAL_RESET_PENDING` continues or re-sends the pending `CREDENTIAL` operation (`QĐ-47a`); `NOT_ACTIVATED` is an integrity anomaly not reachable by any defined operation — incident procedure (maintenance guide), no recovery mode applies (_annotation F11, COORDINATOR chốt kỹ thuật 04/10/2026 (trong phạm vi Owner giao ở QĐ-47; không phải quyết định nghiệp vụ)_). Both phases lock the recipient's `employees` row `FOR SHARE` after the identity rows, as the transfer does (design §9.1, F1). Phase 1 (only if the recipient has no `ACTIVE` + `ACTIVATED` account; `QĐ-47f`, the Owner-approved controlled exception to `QĐ-46a(3)`, Owner via prompt 2026-10-04, not GVHD): create the recipient's `INVITED` account from their working, successor-eligible employee record and send the normal invitation — **no role is granted**; the previous holder keeps the grant. Phase 2 (when the recipient has activated, or in the same run if they already qualify): the recipient must satisfy the eligibility of decision 5 except `currentPassword`; revoke the holder's grant (`revoked_by` empty, actor kind `SYSTEM`) **before** inserting the recipient's (`granted_by` null = system, audit actor `SYSTEM` with the `approvalRef`; completing phase 2 supersedes every other `PENDING` `REASSIGN` operation of the organization — _annotation, W-13/W-15, COORDINATOR chốt kỹ thuật 04/10/2026 (trong phạm vi Owner giao ở QĐ-47; không phải quyết định nghiệp vụ)_); revoke both accounts' sessions and tokens. Activating an invitation never grants the role. A terminated employee is never reactivated and the script changes no employee status. This also answers "no usable Super Admin and no eligible recipient yet" without an endpoint that could grant the role.
   - **`CREDENTIAL`** — holder `ACTIVE`, employee working, but cannot sign in (password **and** mailbox lost). In one transaction: remove the holder's credential row (the account is `ACTIVATED` without a credential: reset pending), revoke sessions/tokens, increment `credentialVersion`, create the `recovery_operations` row, issue a `RECOVER_CREDENTIAL` token (2 hours) and mail it to the login email or to `--new-login-email` when the mailbox is lost. **`loginEmail` and the verification label do not change until the link is redeemed.** Redeeming it (`POST /auth/reset-password`) creates the credential, completes the operation and — when a new address was given and is still unique — sets `loginEmail` to it and records `emailVerificationSource = RECOVERY_REDEEMED` for **that** address; the label of the old address is never carried over. Replacing `loginEmail` is the **only** exception to its immutability: only in this mode, only when the mailbox is lost, only with an approver-confirmed out-of-band identity check (`--identity-check-ref`), only to an address unique in the organization, with both addresses recorded masked, a notice to the old address when it still exists, and no HTTP route. Redeeming does not log the person in.
   - **Replay.** The same `approvalRef` is `ALREADY_APPLIED` when completed; while pending with an unredeemed link it re-issues the link for the **same** recipient and destination (previous token `SUPERSEDED`, previous outbox row `CANCELLED`); a changed recipient/destination/mode under the same ref is refused; a **new** `approvalRef` for the same recipient supersedes the pending operation. Recovery, transfer, lock, termination, password change and approval-holder writes serialize on `IDENTITY` and each re-reads the current holder.
9. **Holder leaving (WP-04 / B6) — `QĐ-47b`.** Handover or termination of the employee whose account holds the grant returns warning `SUPER_ADMIN_HOLDER_LEAVING` (replaces `LAST_USABLE_SUPER_ADMIN`). It never blocks (`QĐ-41b`) and no two-person approval exists; the system does not transfer on anyone's behalf. Controls: the warning is audited with a `superAdminHolder` flag; while the holder's employee is in handover, `GET /me` carries `continuity.holderLeaving = true` with the last working date, and once that date has passed with the account still usable `continuity.handoverPastLastDay = true` — a banner, never a loss of access (blocking is by **status**, not date: `QĐ-41a`); the IT attention list shows the pending transfer; the runbook order is "transfer first, then terminate"; emergency termination is handled by `REASSIGN`.
10. **Monitoring — one predicate, three separate outcomes.** The partial unique index enforces **at most one** grant, not **at least one**: a direct SQL revoke could leave zero. `scripts/identity-continuity-check.ts` (read-only) and a structured `identity.continuity` log event at API/worker start-up and every 15 minutes report: `NO_HOLDER` (no holder — integrity incident, exit 2); `HOLDER_UNUSABLE` with reason in {`EMPLOYEE_TERMINATED`, `ACCOUNT_LOCKED`, `NOT_ACTIVATED`, `CREDENTIAL_RESET_PENDING`} (exit 3; usable = account `ACTIVE` ∧ `ACTIVATED` ∧ credential present ∧ employee not `TERMINATED`); and otherwise healthy (exit 0) **with warnings** `HOLDER_LEAVING` and `HANDOVER_PAST_LAST_DAY` — a handover whose last working day has passed while the account is **still usable** is a warning, never `HOLDER_UNUSABLE` (the earlier reason `EMPLOYEE_IN_HANDOVER_PAST_LAST_DAY` is removed). A second query counts active `SUPER_ADMIN` grants (more than one: `MULTIPLE_HOLDERS`, exit 2). The state is **not** part of `/health/ready`. Direct SQL writes to `user_role_grants` are restricted operationally and audited by recovery; the index alone is not claimed to prevent all administrative corruption.
11. **Removed with this ADR:** `IDENTITY.LAST_SUPER_ADMIN`, any `countOtherActiveSuperAdmins`-style repository method and `assertNotLastSuperAdmin`, and OpenAPI text that mentions them. The HEAD source (`78cb675`) has none of these (the earlier slice was restored out of the working tree), so this is a rule against re-introducing them; tests or fixtures needing **two** Super Admins are not written; no second demo Super Admin is created.
12. **Seed.** Bootstrap grants exactly one `SUPER_ADMIN`; the demo fixture keeps one.

## Alternatives considered

### Application check only, no database index

Rejected: a race or a direct SQL write can create a second holder; the Owner asked for an invariant of the data.

### Two-step transfer (offer, then the successor accepts)

Pros: protects against transferring to someone who did not agree. Cons: a pending state with two possible holders, expiry and cancel rules; more surface than S0 needs; the activation and active-employee preconditions already reduce the risk. Rejected for S0; revisit if the Owner wants it.

### Let the sole Super Admin "step down" and rely on recovery

Leaves the organization with no Super Admin and forces an out-of-application procedure for an ordinary handover; contradicts `QĐ-39c(1)`. Rejected.

### Transfer as an ordinary grant followed by a revoke

Not atomic — a window with two holders or none. Rejected.

### Treat a locked or terminated holder as a vacancy

Opens the role to an out-of-process grant, which the Owner excluded (`QĐ-44b`). Rejected.

### Use bootstrap as the recovery tool

Bootstrap creates an organization and a Super Admin from nothing and must refuse when one exists; making it modify an existing account would turn the installation command into a general bypass. Rejected.

### Credential recovery through an in-application email change

An in-application "change my login email" operation cannot help the case that matters (the holder has lost the mailbox **and** the password) and would add an account-takeover surface to every account. Rejected for the Super Admin recovery case; the email-change exception exists only in the offline command (`QĐ-47a`).

### A second recovery channel stored at bootstrap (verified recovery email)

Avoids the offline procedure but creates a second credential channel to protect and a second place where the identity of the holder can be forged. **Rejected** — the Owner chose offline `CREDENTIAL` recovery (`QĐ-47a`).

### Two-person approval to terminate the holder's employee

Adds a workflow, an approver who is neither IT nor Super Admin (only the Spending Approver is a candidate) and a way to block a legitimate HR action. **Rejected** by the Owner (`QĐ-47b`).

## Consequences

- Supported bootstrap and atomic transfer/recovery preserve exactly one holder at committed boundaries. The partial unique index enforces **at most one**, not **at least one**; the continuity check and operational restriction cover the rest.
- A locked or terminated holder blocks all role changes until recovery. That is operationally heavier and is the Owner's explicit choice (`QĐ-44b`).
- The holder's account cannot be locked in the application: only a Super Admin can lock accounts and `QĐ-39c(1)` forbids locking one's own. Transfer first, then the new holder locks the old account.
- A mistaken transfer to an eligible but wrong person cannot be undone by the former holder (no longer Super Admin); the new holder transfers back, or `REASSIGN` recovery applies if the new holder is not usable (Docs `PD-2`).
- The transfer serializes with every other identity write through the `IDENTITY` lock, including handover and termination (which also take `IDENTITY`, design §9.1). It takes no `STRUCTURE` lock; it can still wait on, or delay, an employee write that updates the successor's `employees` row, through its `FOR SHARE` on that row (design §9.1a barrier case 5). By design analysis an import commit creates employees and updates no existing employee row, so the transfer is not expected to wait on an import — a prediction to be confirmed by the N19 barrier cases on real PostgreSQL, not an established fact.
- The migration needs a pre-flight on any database that may already hold two grants.
- The transfer endpoint is an authorization-sensitive surface: refusals are logged at `warn` with `appUserId` and reason only; no e-mail, no token.
- Credential recovery adds a `recovery_operations` table, the action-token purpose `RECOVER_CREDENTIAL`, the `emailVerificationSource` value `RECOVERY_REDEEMED` and the revocation reason `RECOVERY`; they are created by the single B1b migration. The monitor reports `CREDENTIAL_RESET_PENDING`.
- The recovery command's separation of duties is a **declared-code, procedural control**; it is not evidence that a real approval happened. Operations must keep the written approval and restrict who may run the script.
